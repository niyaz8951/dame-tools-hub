Plot the air, join the dots, read the duty.

Enter as many air states as your system has — outdoor, mixed, off-coil, supply, room — and each one is plotted and joined to the next. You get every property of each state, what each step does to the air, the sensible and latent split, and the apparatus dew point and bypass factor a cooling coil would need. Properties follow the ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals, Chapter 1, including the water-vapour enhancement factor, so the figures match ASHRAE Table 2 and Chart No. 1.

    How the numbers are worked out

    • Saturation pressure of pure water is the Hyland and Wexler correlation given as equations 5 and 6 of ASHRAE Fundamentals chapter 1 — equation 5 over ice below 0 °C, equation 6 over liquid water above it.
    • The water-vapour enhancement factor f (Greenspan's fit of the Hyland–Wexler real-gas formulation, about 1.004 at sea level) multiplies that saturation pressure wherever it is used — saturation humidity ratio, relative humidity, dew point and wet bulb. This is what ASHRAE uses to compute Table 2 and to draw Chart No. 1; the plain perfect-gas equations leave it out and read 0.4 to 0.6% low on humidity ratio, which is the gap between the earlier version of this tool and the printed chart. Checked against Table 2 from -20 to 50 °C: humidity ratio agrees to within 0.03%, enthalpy to within 0.1%.
    • Humidity ratio, relative humidity, enthalpy and specific volume follow equations 20, 24, 30 and 26. Wet bulb uses equations 33 and 35, the Handbook relation, rather than the psychrometer approximation that most quick tools carry — below about 30% relative humidity the two disagree by more than a gram per kilogram.
    • Dew point and wet bulb are found by bisecting the defining relation rather than from a fitted polynomial, so they carry no fit error and cannot fail to converge.
    • Density is the density of the moist air mixture, (1+W)/v. Specific volume is stated per kilogram of dry air, so reporting 1/v as density reads about 2% low at 35 °C and 60% relative humidity.
    • Barometric pressure follows the standard atmosphere, equation 3. Altitude is not cosmetic: at Riyadh's 612 m the same relative humidity carries about 7% more moisture than at sea level, which moves every coil load on the page. The chart is redrawn for the pressure in use and says so in its caption.
    • Airflow is taken as the volume flow at the state it leaves, which is where a fan or a flow station measures it, so the dry air mass flow is V/v at that state. Total load is the enthalpy change, sensible uses the moist air specific heat 1.006 + 1.86W at the mean humidity ratio, and latent is the remainder — so sensible and latent always add up to total.
    • Mixing follows equations 45 and 46: humidity ratio and enthalpy are mass-averaged on dry air, and the mixed dry bulb follows from those. A mixed row is a stored figure: if its two sources are changed afterwards the page says so, and it is mixed again to update it. The step into a mixed row is drawn dashed from both sources and carries no load.
    • The sensible heat ratio is left blank when the total load is close to zero (evaporative cooling), where the ratio would be a very large number with no meaning.
    • In IP the same SI equations are used and the figures are converted for display: °F, gr/lb, Btu/lb (zero at 0 °F dry air), ft³/lb, in.Hg, MBH and cfm.
    • The apparatus dew point is where the process line, extended past the leaving state, meets the saturation curve; the bypass factor is (t₂ − t_adp) / (t₁ − t_adp). When the line never reaches saturation there is no apparatus dew point, and the tool says so rather than inventing one — that result means a single coil cannot produce that leaving condition from that entering condition.
    • The comfort zone overlay is indicative only. ASHRAE Standard 55 depends on air speed, clothing, metabolic rate and radiant temperature, none of which a chart can know, so treat the shaded band as orientation rather than a compliance check.
    • Your states are kept in this browser only, under your user name, and are restored the next time you open the page. Nothing from this tool is saved to the hub database.
