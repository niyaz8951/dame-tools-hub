How the numbers are worked out

  • The centre of gravity is the weighted average of each block's own centre. A block is treated as uniform, so its centre sits at the middle of its size.
  • With one support it carries everything. With two, the load splits along the line between them by lever arm.
  • With three or more, the unit is treated as rigid and the supports as equally stiff, so the loads form a plane through the centre of gravity. Three supports give an exact answer; four or more are statically indeterminate, and this is the usual first-pass assumption.
  • A negative load means the unit is lifting off that support rather than pressing on it. With redistribution on, that support is dropped and the rest are solved again.
  • Tipping is checked against the outline drawn around the supports. The margin is the shortest distance from the centre of gravity to the edge of that outline.
  • The sample projects carry indicative weights only. Real units also carry pipework, refrigerant charge, water content, and the effect of wind or seismic load — treat every result here as a first pass to check against certified weights.
  • Your project is kept in this browser only, and is restored the next time you open the page. Nothing is uploaded.
